FOR EXAMINERS & JUDGES

What NER Sentinel does

A quick self-guided tour — read this instead of a live walkthrough.

CITIZEN ROLE

👤 What a citizen can do

  • Dashboard / Risk map — see every monitored zone's live computed risk, ranked by priority, with the exact reason each one is flagged.
  • Field report — file a hazard report with GPS location and photo/video evidence, sent straight to the official verification queue.
  • Safety status — one-tap "I'm Safe" / "I Need Help", visible live to officials, in the citizen's own language.
  • History — browse real historical NER landslide events for context on what "high risk" has actually meant here before.

OFFICIAL ROLE

🏛️ What an official can do

  • Command center — every zone ranked by computed priority, with AI risk, population impact, and road status in one table.
  • Report queue — verify or reject citizen reports, and attach the official's own field-verification evidence.
  • Needs-help list — a live feed of every citizen who has flagged themselves as needing help, with their last known location.
  • Alert trigger — send a real multilingual SMS warning for any zone, in as many of the 7 supported languages as needed.

DATA SOURCES — REAL, NOT SIMULATED

Where every number comes from

Weather & rainfallOpen-Meteo (live forecast + hourly precipitation)
Satellite rainfallNASA POWER — public, no-key satellite/reanalysis precipitation
Terrain & slopeOpenTopoData elevation API
Landslide riskTrained ML model, served by our own FastAPI microservice
Historical incidents17 real, publicly-reported NER events, 2022–2026
SMS alertsTwilio (sends for real once configured; otherwise shows the exact message)

Two figures are documented approximations, not measured: population estimate (nearest zone's density × area, not a gridded census layer) and road status (a heuristic derived from priority level — no live traffic feed exists for this region). Both are labeled as such everywhere they appear.

OFFICIAL WORKSPACE — HOW THE RESPONSE TOOLS WORK

Beyond the dashboard

  • Real headcount vs. population estimate — the dashboard's population number is a density-based estimate. The Command Center's "Citizens reporting near a zone" is a real, exact count of people who actually used the app or SMS to check in.
  • Possibly affected / went quiet — the honest substitute for cell-tower tracking (which needs telecom-carrier access no hackathon can get). It only sees people who have used NER Sentinel at least once, and treats "no update since the event" as a signal to check on them — not proof of anything.
  • Reply SAFE / HELP by SMS, no app — a citizen registers their phone once (in Safety Status), then can reply to any alert by plain text. Requires a Twilio number with its webhook pointed at this server's public URL (via ngrok for a local demo) — the code is real and tested, but needs that one setup step to actually receive replies.
  • Multi-agency dispatch — Police / Fire / Medical / Disaster Management are separate SMS recipient lists, each read from your own .env. They are never pre-filled with a real public emergency number — you point them at your own team's phones for a demo.
  • Responder routing — real road-network routing via OSRM (OpenStreetMap's routing engine), not a straight line on a map. "Low-risk" means the route that stays furthest from other high-risk zones, among OSRM's real alternative routes — it is not live traffic or road-closure data, which doesn't exist as a free source for this region.
